# Hardware examination plan

## Safety first

Do **not** connect the arriving Multiphone to mains power before inspection.
Treat it as an archaeological/electrical object until its wiring and insulation
are understood.

## Initial documentation

Photograph the exterior, underside, chassis, transformer, terminal strips,
relays, coin mechanism, microphone/transducer, speaker, lamps, wiring, external
connections, and model/serial markings.

Number every position on the large internal terminal strip before changing
anything.

## Electrical mapping

With power disconnected, use continuity testing to identify connections to:

- coin switch and relay contacts/coils;
- microphone/transducer and speaker;
- lamps and transformer;
- external terminals and unidentified sockets;
- chassis and other destinations.

Measure voltage, AC/DC character, duration, and polarity only after a safe test
plan exists. Modern electronics should connect through optical or relay
isolation, preferably using removable screw-terminal harnesses.

## Current hypotheses

The purchased machine appears likely to be a postwar nickel-era unit, but this
is not established. Existing photographs suggest an AC cord, Stancor transformer,
substantial terminal strip, external two-terminal block, and an unidentified
socket. Their functions are **TBD**.
